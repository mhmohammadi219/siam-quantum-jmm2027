Optimization by Decoded Quantum Interferometry

Stephen P. Jordan · Google Quantum AI

Abstract

Decoded Quantum Interferometry (DQI) is a quantum optimization framework that uses quantum Fourier transforms to convert certain optimization tasks into decoding problems. The talk discusses how algebraic or sparse structure in an objective function can be reflected in an associated decoding problem, giving a route to large quantum speedups in structured settings and motivating further study of harder combinatorial problems such as sparse max-XORSAT.

Quantum Score Matching and Its Application to Gibbs State Learning

Yulong Dong and Jiaqi Leng · University of Michigan; Virginia Tech

Abstract

This work develops a quantum analogue of score matching for learning quantum states described by noncommuting density operators. Applied to Gibbs-state learning, the framework avoids additional thermal-state preparation and achieves optimal sample complexity in a high-temperature regime under locality assumptions. Numerical and hardware experiments indicate that the method can remain effective with imperfect gradient estimates and on noisy quantum devices.

Idempotent Channels and Metastability in Dissipative Quantum Dynamics

Hao-En Li, Lin Lin, and Michael Ragone · University of California, Berkeley

Abstract

The talk studies metastable open quantum dynamics through approximately idempotent quantum channels. The underlying framework constructs a reduced physical state space that preserves the slowly evolving information, including both classical sectors and coherent quantum blocks. It provides quantitative approximation guarantees and a reduced transition channel that can be iterated to predict long-time microscopic dynamics with controlled error.
11:00 AM

An Improved Quantum Algorithm for Convex-Body Volume Estimation

Ruizhe Zhang · Purdue University

Abstract

Estimating the volume of a high-dimensional convex body is a fundamental problem in computational geometry and theoretical computer science. This work improves the quantum query complexity for relative-error volume estimation using new analyses of quantum walks and a quantum estimator for products of normalizer ratios arising in simulated annealing. It also establishes a stronger quantum query lower bound for constant relative error.

1:00 PM

Efficient Quantum Algorithms for Linear Matrix Equations and Applications

Rolando D. Somma · Google Quanutm AI

Abstract

The talk concerns quantum algorithms for matrix-valued linear equations and related dynamical problems. Recent work in this direction develops efficient quantum procedures for linear matrix equations and matrix differential equations, with applications including open-system simulation. A central goal is to avoid inefficiencies caused by encoding large matrix solutions only as normalized quantum states and to obtain nearly optimal dependence on relevant accuracy and evolution parameters.
1:30 PM

Quantum Solvers for Nonlinear Matrix Equations in Quantum Chemistry

Pablo Rodenas-Ruiz, Andrew Zhao, and Joonho Lee · Imperial College London; Sandia National Laboratories; Harvard University

Abstract

This work develops quantum algorithms for nonlinear matrix equations arising in quantum chemistry, focusing on algebraic Riccati equations and random-phase approximation methods. Stabilizing solutions are represented through invariant-subspace projectors of an associated non-normal matrix and implemented using contour-integral resolvents and quantum singular value transformation. The framework also supports estimating quantities such as electronic correlation-energy densities.
2:00 PM

Instance Aware Quantum Linear System Algorithms

Jianqiang Li · The University of Texas at Dallas

Abstract

The talk focuses on quantum linear-system algorithms whose performance reflects structure of the particular problem instance rather than depending only on worst-case matrix condition numbers. Such approaches seek to exploit features of the right-hand side, augmented formulations, or other structural information to reduce the effective complexity and broaden the range of linear systems for which quantum methods may provide an advantage.
2:30 PM

State- and Observable-aware Shadow Quantum Singular Value Transformation

Nai-Hui Chia · Rice University

Abstract

Shadow quantum singular value transformation reduces the resources needed when an application requires observable estimates rather than a full implementation of a transformed operator. The approach exploits structure in the input state and target observables, including low-dimensional Krylov subspaces, to lower circuit depth. It also develops a classical-shadow-style representation that can support estimating observables specified after the quantum computation.

3:30 PM

Mathematical Analysis of Many-Body Quantum Simulation with Coulomb Potentials

Di Fang · Duke University

Abstract

This talk studies rigorous complexity and convergence guarantees for simulating many-body quantum systems with singular Coulomb interactions. It shows that first-order Trotterization can retain polynomial dependence on the number of particles in the continuum limit despite the unbounded Hamiltonian, with a sharp quarter-order convergence rate in the general setting. The work also discusses additional regularity conditions and spatial-discretization issues relevant to algorithmic complexity.
